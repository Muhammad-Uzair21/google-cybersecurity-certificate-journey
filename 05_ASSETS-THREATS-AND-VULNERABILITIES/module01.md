# 🛡️ Course 5 — Module 1: Introduction to Asset Security

> Understanding organizational assets, risk management, asset classification, security planning, cybersecurity frameworks, and practical risk assessment.

---

## 🎯 Core Security Concepts

### Risk

Anything that impacts the **Confidentiality, Integrity, or Availability (CIA)** of an asset.

```text
Likelihood × Impact = Risk
```

Risk management helps organizations:
- Prevent costly incidents
- Prioritize critical assets
- Improve systems and processes
- Determine acceptable levels of risk

### Threat

Any circumstance or event capable of negatively impacting an asset.

**Types:**
- Intentional
- Unintentional

### Vulnerability

A weakness that can be exploited by a threat.

**Categories:**
- Technical vulnerabilities
- Human vulnerabilities

---

## 📦 Asset Management

The process of tracking organizational assets and the risks affecting them.

Includes:
- Asset inventory
- Asset classification

### 🏷️ Asset Classification

| Classification | Description |
|---|---|
| Restricted | Highest sensitivity |
| Confidential | Significant impact if disclosed |
| Internal Only | Accessible internally |
| Public | Safe for public release |

Classification considers:
- What the asset is
- Where it is
- Who owns it
- Its business importance

### ⚠️ Risk Classifications

Common impacts of security incidents:
- Damage
- Disclosure
- Loss of information

---

## 📋 Elements of a Security Plan

Every security plan is built around:

- **Policies** — high-level rules and expectations
- **Standards** — mandatory requirements that support policies
- **Procedures** — step-by-step instructions for carrying out security tasks

---

## 🛡️ NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

A voluntary framework consisting of standards, guidelines, and best practices for managing cybersecurity risks.

### Components

- **Core** — security functions and activities
- **Tier** — level of cybersecurity maturity
- **Profile** — aligns security activities with business requirements

## 📌 Module Takeaways

- Assets need to be identified and classified before they can be properly protected.
- Risk depends on both likelihood and impact.
- Threats exploit vulnerabilities to affect assets.
- Security plans turn organizational security requirements into actionable controls and procedures.
- Frameworks such as NIST CSF help organizations manage cybersecurity risk systematically.
